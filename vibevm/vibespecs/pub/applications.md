## From a recurring omission to a useful aid {#from-a-recurring-omission-to-a-useful-aid}

An organizer repeatedly receives physically complete seminar cards containing wording from an earlier exercise. The facilitator can explain the exercise, and the packer can count and inspect cards; neither currently sees the whole question at the useful moment.

Begin with [CHK.1](spec://ai.lev/fpf-eng-chk/contract/CHK-1#root) if useful questions are missing. The first result is a question set tied to the work, or a clear statement of the domain judgement still needed. With adequate questions already available, start at the contribution that is missing. The mantra below follows the aid through use and appraisal; a sufficient direct question can end the search immediately.

> **Mantra — from omission to a worthwhile aid:** Name the omission and the work result it threatens. Reuse adequate questions, forms and checking means; obtain only what is missing. Tie each question to its subject, description and intended use. Make an answer possible at the useful moment, challenge uncertain criteria or means, and answer from adequate grounds. Keep the answer, the decision and the work distinct. Connect answers that affect the whole. After change or interruption, recover the question and its grounds; reconsider only affected reliance. Compare the aid's contribution with its full burden. Improve what obstructs useful work, or retire the aid when a simpler arrangement suffices.

The worked case below explains those conditional moves. Resume at the unresolved contribution after interruption; if the questions have been answered but the aid's worth remains unknown, continue with **Worth and improvement** and [ME.14][ME14].

**Selection.** CHK.1 separates the physical-set description from the assumption that this set will serve the exercise. The three selected questions concern contents/readability, wording and assembly. A separate exercise trial addresses instructional usefulness. The result is a bounded question set with actual sources for its answers.

**Construction.** CHK.2 puts the set and description reference once above the questions. The packing view carries count/readability and assembly; the facilitator's view carries wording comparison. Both retain S and v2. The answer forms allow a supported negative or an unresolved question. The transposed example in CHK.2 shows the same answers without changing their subject.

**Fitting.** CHK.3 puts the questions in the preparation workspace, with the description accessible before the box closes. The packer reports physical conditions; the facilitator compares wording. An assisted first use reveals an ambiguous readability condition, which returns to criterion construction and help.

**Use and change.** CHK.4 supplies answers from observations at 10:00, 10:05 and 10:10, entered at 10:12. At 10:15 the description changes to v3. Known unchanged v2 cards already mismatch it. If current cards may differ, current wording is instead unknown. The facilitator decides to keep v3 and replace the affected card, then obtains the changed comparison and any physical checks affected by handling. The old v2 answer remains historical.

**Worth and improvement.** A representative trial through [ME.11][ME11] observes whether the aid catches the wording problem, whether users can interpret the questions, and how much time or duplicated work it adds. [ME.14][ME14] compares that contribution with burden across the preparation work. A timely wording check can avoid reopening, replacing and repacking the set; completing the checklist faster is no gain if it misses that problem or merely moves the delay elsewhere. If a direct conversation at packing already prevents the omission with less cost, retain the conversation and retire the redundant aid. If the aid is useful but its description is hard to find, [ME.16][ME16] selects that focused improvement rather than enlarging the list.

Keep use, detected problems and work outcomes distinct. A caught wording mismatch is an observed contribution; it does not establish that the exercise would otherwise have failed. Another participant might have caught it later. Conversely, a session that went well does not establish that every check was sound. When appraising the change, account for the training, support, other changes and case differences accompanying the aid. Staff enthusiasm or a rising count of ticks does not isolate its effect.

The worked route uses CHK.1–4 with Method engineering; uncertain criteria or shared reliance bring in CHK.6 or CHK.5 when needed. It can return at several points: unsupported usefulness calls for domain inquiry; lost references call for template repair; inaccessible information calls for fitting; unchanged adequate grounds support economical reuse. The three physical-set answers never settle the exercise's usefulness by themselves.

## Shared work with uncertain criteria and changed premises {#shared-work-with-uncertain-criteria-and-changed-premises}

A rented-room workshop combines projection, card exercises and participant coordination. The technician uses a venue tool, the facilitator a materials workspace, and the organizer a session calendar. The team is uncertain whether “readable on screen” can be judged reliably at the technician's desk.

When local answers exist but their support for the session is unclear, [CHK.5](spec://ai.lev/fpf-eng-chk/contract/CHK-5#root) first connects them to the shared result and exposes missing or conflicting contributions. Follow the return that answers the actual difficulty. In this case, uncertain projection checking calls for qualification before reliance on its verdict.

> **Mantra — from local answers to a shared result:** Name the shared result. For each contribution, recover the subject, question, conditions and grounds behind its answer. Reuse what is adequate; qualify uncertain checks and obtain what is missing. Ask what the whole still needs and whether the contributions can combine. Return a missing performer or resource to coordination, an unanswered subject question to the needed observation or interpretation, and incompatible claims to those who can resolve them. After change, retain unaffected grounds and reconsider dependent questions; after interruption, recover these distinct next moves. Use a conversation or an existing view when enough; record more only for a concrete later reliance.

The worked case below connects these moves to the room, materials and workshop. Stop reconciling when the shared question is sufficiently answered; the participants still make the continuation decision and perform the selected work.

**Qualification and selection.** CHK.6 challenges the criterion under the intended viewing conditions. A representative participant examines the actual projected example from relevant seats. If desk readability fails to distinguish a real difficulty, the team changes that criterion or its checking means. CHK.1 then retains questions about the room setup, actual content and exercise use; it leaves unrelated venue questions in the venue's own practice.

**Local representation and occasion.** CHK.2 preserves the session, room and materials references in the relevant local views. CHK.3 arranges the observation while the room and actual content are available. A missing capable interpreter returns to capability support; an unavailable room returns to scheduling.

**Local answers and simultaneous contribution.** Through CHK.4, the technician establishes equipment conditions and the facilitator compares projected and physical examples. The actual projection check contributes to equipment preparation and preparation of the workshop at the same time. Its relation to the whole changes what is checked: a test image alone cannot establish the actual example's use.

**Reconciliation.** CHK.5 connects the local answers to workshop W. A whole-session account includes the actual materials and arrangement, not a count of green local lists. If the facilitator and technician need the room at incompatible times, [OPS.4][OPS4] makes the conflicting claims visible to the people who can select a feasible arrangement before those contributions combine.

**Changed premise and continuation.** Moving W from room A to B reopens the room-dependent observations. Revising the example reopens its content and display questions. Known unaffected card facts remain usable. An absent technician assignment returns to coordination; an assigned technician with an unknown projection result returns to observation. After an interruption, the shared account lets participants recover these different next moves.

The route uses all six Checklist Methods because this constructed case has uncertain criteria, local forms, an occasion, actual answers and shared reliance. A simpler workshop may need only a conversation and a direct check. The example explains the connections; it does not prescribe six phases for every session.
